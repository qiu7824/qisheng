# Prepared audio effects

Effects preparation owns allocation, FFT planning, resource validation and cancellation. Render operations use fixed storage and carry the original media coordinate. An impulse resource identifier is metadata; loading its bytes requires its normal selected-file or bundled-resource owner.

`ImpulseBuilder` reserves the complete admitted PCM capacity before decoding writes any frames. Responses have one, two or four channels, a sample rate from 8 kHz to 384 kHz and a duration of at most four seconds. Encoded-file byte limits belong to the resource loader; decoded PCM and every live FFT bank share the process-wide 64 MiB DSP reservation. Preparation rejects configurations that exceed that budget.

`ImpulseEncodedInput` reserves an encoded buffer of at most 8 MiB before selected-file bytes are appended. The current IR decoder accepts little-endian RIFF/WAVE PCM and native FLAC; actual container magic and codec are checked, rather than trusting the extension hint. `decode_impulse` accepts that owner and an `ImpulseDecodeContext`, with a positive deadline of at most 120 seconds. The decoder checks cancellation and the deadline during bounded reads, seeks, packets and finalization; it rejects changing layouts, oversized packets, truncated declared lossless audio and responses longer than four seconds. FLAC block allocation and codec private metadata are checked before decoder creation. The decoder creates no paths, listeners or filesystem grants.

`prepare_selected_impulse` reads from an already live `AuthorizedFile`. It retains and rechecks the original selection, actual file identity, byte length and change stamp during bounded reads and after decoding. A second complete read verifies that the whole SHA-256 still matches the decoded representation. The returned PCM and receipt metadata do not grant further filesystem access, and loading creates no sidecar or replacement file.

`PreparedConvolution` uses 512-frame partitions and 1,024-point FFTs. One/two-channel responses apply separate left/right filters; four-channel responses use LL/LR/RL/RR routing. The wet output contains two channels even when the source or device is mono. Additional native wet lanes are zero. Mono device folding occurs after the spatial stage, while additional dry lanes require the same admitted delay.

The convolution bank reports a 512-frame delay for both normal input and short EOF. Initial alignment padding is invalid output and has no media stamp. Delayed original audio retains its exact stamp; acoustic tail is valid audio with `tail:true` and no media stamp. Tail does not advance the original song clock. Empty EOF emits nothing. Invalid stamps, wrong stream, non-finite input, finite overflow and exhausted counters are rejected before accepting another input frame.

Normalized responses use the pinned Chromium RMS floor, −58 dB calibration, 44,100 Hz rate compensation and half gain for true stereo. Preparation resamples with a ratio-scaled band-limited window. Numerical execution and callback timing are required before advertising a supported IR/rate/layout combination; coefficient frequency analysis alone does not establish actual render performance.

`PreparedEffectMix` keeps dry samples and stamps in a fixed ring matching the wet delay. It compares their original stamps before combining `dry × main_gain + wet × send_gain`. Gains are finite and range from zero to five. Native extra dry channels retain their gain and delay. The bank exposes at least two effect lanes so a mono output adapter can fold both stereo wet channels.

`PreparedCompressor` follows the pinned Chromium default curve, linked stereo detector, 32-frame envelope subdivision and delayed output. Native extra channels pass with the same delay. Padding and acoustic tail remain distinct from stamped original media. The spatial stage reads prepared coefficients from a control-owned monotonic phase and applies fixed matrix interpolation; device conversion and final gain follow it.

Source replacement, seek, cancellation and device switching must reject earlier epochs. Pause retains pending samples without applying synthetic zero frames. EOF drains admitted wet and compressor storage before retiring the owner. Candidate plans and buffers must be reserved before closing a working output device; callback retirement returns them to their actual owner.

`EffectAdmission` correlates the latest desired sequence with an opaque preparation ticket and the actual stream epoch/format. Cancellation makes a ticket ineligible for installation, but its slot remains occupied until its preparation owner reports actual drop. Installing a replacement moves the old bank to retirement; a third bank is rejected until callback handback and destruction are acknowledged. Shutdown is drained only after candidate, active and retiring owners have all completed their actual release.

`EffectsControl` and `EffectsRenderExchange` move prepared banks through bounded SPSC queues. The render side returns stale banks to the owning control side; it does not free them in a callback. Live replacement waits while the old bank still owns unpresented media or a deferred final frame. The control side decrements the bank count only after actual destruction. Callback shutdown must return the complete render owner before closing its exchange on the engine or device owner.

`AudioPlayer::set_effects(settings, impulse)` admits validated preferences with the actual immutable `OwnedImpulse`. The resource identifier must match that owner; an identifier alone cannot activate a response. Spatial changes use the same desired graph and retain its selected impulse. Output preparation uses the counted factory and the current stream token, source epoch, sample rate and layout.

Before attaching the first PCM source, the engine configures the desired graph. A requested convolution or nondefault gain bank holds source consumption until a matching bank reaches the callback; waiting output is silence and does not advance the media clock. The legacy spatial-only seek path retains its established device folding behavior.

`effects_snapshot()` separates the desired sequence from actual presentation. Fixed callback atomics publish the presented bank's sequence, format, latency, remaining tail, transition, compressor reduction and bank storage bytes. These bytes describe that prepared graph; decoded response owners, preparation records and handoff storage also retain their independent process-wide DSP reservations. Shutdown releases the engine's response owner after the actual engine and cache join.

The public player and output interfaces support initial owned-response activation. Backend/CLI resource import, durable response storage, persistent convolution preferences and the live replacement policy for an already populated graph remain separate implementation and acceptance work.
